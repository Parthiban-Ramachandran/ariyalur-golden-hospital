How Care May Work

How patient care may progress

The following illustrative examples explain how patients may be assessed, treated and supported during recovery. The exact investigations, treatment and outcome will vary depending on each patient's condition and the treating clinician's assessment.

Emergency & Trauma Care · Orthopaedics

Emergency care for a suspected fracture

01

A patient is brought to the casualty department after a road accident with severe pain and a suspected fracture in one leg.

24/7 CasualtyCT ScanC-ArmICU
Orthopaedics department
  1. 1

    Before treatment

    • The patient may have severe pain, swelling and difficulty moving or bearing weight on the injured leg.
    • The casualty team assesses the injury and stabilises the patient.
    • X-rays, CT scans or other investigations may be requested based on the suspected injuries.
    • Blood grouping, cross-matching and other preoperative tests may be performed when required.
  2. 2

    Treatment provided

    • Pain relief, immobilisation and other immediate supportive care may be provided.
    • The fracture may be treated conservatively using a cast, brace or traction.
    • Surgery may be recommended when clinically necessary.
    • Anaesthesia and C-Arm imaging may support the surgical procedure.
    • After surgery, the patient may be monitored in the recovery area, ward or ICU based on clinical need.
  3. 3

    After treatment

    • Physiotherapy may begin during admission when clinically appropriate.
    • Mobility and weight-bearing are gradually increased according to the surgeon's and physiotherapist's instructions.
    • Follow-up consultations and repeat imaging may be advised to assess healing.
    • Referral or transfer may be required when specialised treatment or additional resources are needed.

The actual investigations, treatment and recovery period are decided by the treating clinician after an individual assessment.

Orthopaedics · Physiotherapy

Care for advanced knee arthritis

02

A patient with long-standing knee pain and reduced mobility is assessed for advanced knee arthritis.

Operating TheatreBlood BankPhysiotherapy
Surgical services
  1. 1

    Before treatment

    • The patient may experience persistent knee pain, stiffness, swelling and difficulty walking or climbing stairs.
    • An orthopaedic consultation and imaging review are performed.
    • Medication, physiotherapy and other non-surgical treatments may be reviewed.
    • Preoperative investigations are selected according to the patient's health, planned procedure and anaesthetic requirements.
    • The insurance desk may assist with documentation, subject to insurer or scheme approval.
  2. 2

    Treatment provided

    • Non-surgical options such as medication, activity modification and physiotherapy may be recommended initially.
    • Joint replacement surgery may be advised when clinically indicated.
    • The procedure, possible risks, benefits and alternative treatment options are discussed with the patient.
    • Post-operative monitoring, pain management and infection-prevention measures are provided.
  3. 3

    After treatment

    • Physiotherapy begins when the treating team considers it clinically appropriate.
    • The patient gradually works towards improved movement, strength and daily activity.
    • Exercise and home-care instructions are explained to the patient and family.
    • Review consultations are scheduled according to the treating team's advice.
    • Recovery time and mobility improvement vary between patients.

The actual treatment and outcome depend on the patient's condition, overall health and response to treatment.

Urology · Scans & Imaging

Care for suspected kidney stones

03

A patient presents with severe flank pain, vomiting, fever or blood in the urine that may be related to a kidney stone.

UltrasoundCT ScanEndoscopy Suite
Urology department
  1. 1

    Before treatment

    • The patient may experience sudden severe pain in the back, side or lower abdomen.
    • Fever, nausea, vomiting or urinary discomfort may also be present.
    • The clinician assesses the patient's symptoms and medical condition.
    • Urine tests, blood tests, ultrasound or CT imaging may be recommended.
    • Specialist availability is confirmed when urology consultation is required.
  2. 2

    Treatment provided

    • Pain relief, hydration guidance, observation or medication may be provided.
    • Drainage or another procedure may be required if there is obstruction, infection or persistent pain.
    • A urologist may recommend a procedure based on the stone's size, location and the patient's condition.
    • Available treatment options, risks and alternatives are discussed with the patient.
    • Treatment may be provided as day care or through inpatient admission.
  3. 3

    After treatment

    • Pain and urinary symptoms are monitored after treatment.
    • Repeat imaging or kidney-function testing may be advised.
    • The treating clinician may provide guidance regarding fluid intake and diet.
    • Follow-up appointments are arranged according to the patient's clinical needs.
    • Additional treatment may be necessary if the stone has not completely passed or been removed.

The treatment method and recovery period vary according to the stone and the patient's overall condition.

Nephrology · Dialysis Unit

Maintenance dialysis care

04

A patient with chronic kidney disease is assessed for regular maintenance haemodialysis closer to home.

Dialysis UnitEligible Scheme SupportLaboratory
Insurance & scheme desk
  1. 1

    Before treatment

    • The patient may have reduced kidney function and require ongoing dialysis support.
    • Kidney-function tests and other related investigations are reviewed.
    • A nephrology consultation is conducted to plan dialysis frequency and timing.
    • Dialysis access and the urgency of treatment are assessed.
    • Scheme or insurance eligibility may be reviewed by the hospital support team.
  2. 2

    Treatment provided

    • Maintenance haemodialysis is provided according to an agreed schedule.
    • The patient's blood pressure, weight and general condition are monitored during treatment.
    • Related medical conditions are managed by the appropriate treating clinicians.
    • Hospital staff may assist with eligible scheme documentation and authorisation requests.
    • Referral may be arranged if specialised dialysis access or tertiary care is required.
  3. 3

    After treatment

    • The patient continues regular dialysis according to the nephrologist's advice.
    • Follow-up investigations may be conducted to monitor kidney function and treatment response.
    • Local dialysis services may reduce travel for some patients.
    • Additional specialist review or access surgery may be recommended when clinically indicated.
    • Dialysis schedules and long-term outcomes vary depending on the patient's health and treatment requirements.

Obstetrics & Gynaecology · Paediatrics

Care during an unplanned caesarean birth

05

A pregnant patient in labour is assessed, and an urgent caesarean delivery may be recommended for the safety of the mother or baby.

Emergency surgical supportBlood BankPaediatrics
Obstetrics & Gynaecology
  1. 1

    Before treatment

    • The mother and baby are monitored according to the clinical situation.
    • Maternal health, fetal heart rate and labour progress are assessed.
    • Ultrasound or other investigations may be performed when indicated.
    • The obstetric, anaesthetic, theatre and newborn-care teams are contacted according to clinical need and availability.
    • The reason for recommending a caesarean delivery is discussed with the patient and family wherever circumstances permit.
  2. 2

    Treatment provided

    • A caesarean delivery is performed when recommended by the obstetric team.
    • Anaesthesia and surgical monitoring are provided during the procedure.
    • The newborn is assessed and supported by the available newborn-care team.
    • The mother receives post-operative monitoring, pain management and wound care.
    • Additional care may be provided if complications are identified.
  3. 3

    After treatment

    • The mother is monitored during post-operative recovery.
    • Breastfeeding, mobility and wound-care support are provided.
    • The newborn undergoes appropriate checks before discharge.
    • Immunisation and postnatal follow-up are advised.
    • The mother and family receive discharge instructions and information about warning signs that require medical attention.

The delivery plan, hospital stay and recovery period depend on the clinical condition of the mother and baby.

Emergency Medicine · Intensive Care

Emergency care for suspected pesticide exposure

06

A patient is brought to the emergency department following suspected pesticide exposure or poisoning. Suspected poisoning is a medical emergency — call 94875 76493 or the appropriate public emergency service.

Emergency WardICULaboratory
Emergency care
  1. 1

    Before treatment

    • The patient may have breathing difficulty, vomiting, sweating, confusion, weakness or reduced consciousness.
    • Vomiting should not be induced and home remedies should not be attempted unless instructed by a qualified medical professional.
    • The emergency team assesses the patient's airway, breathing and circulation.
    • Urgent investigations and continuous monitoring may be initiated.
    • The product container or label may be brought to the hospital only when it is safe to do so.
  2. 2

    Treatment provided

    • Immediate stabilisation and supportive emergency care are provided.
    • Treatment may include monitoring, medication, antidotal therapy or respiratory support.
    • Repeated laboratory investigations may be conducted to guide treatment.
    • Ward admission, ICU care or transfer to another centre may be required depending on the substance and severity.
    • The patient's condition is continuously reassessed by the treating team.
  3. 3

    After treatment

    • The patient may be moved to step-down care as the condition stabilises.
    • Ongoing monitoring continues until the treating team considers discharge appropriate.
    • Emotional-health assessment and psychosocial support may be offered when appropriate.
    • The patient and family receive guidance regarding medicines, warning signs and follow-up care.
    • Recovery and long-term effects depend on the substance, exposure level and speed of receiving medical treatment.

In an emergency, contact the hospital or the appropriate public emergency service immediately. Treatment and outcomes vary depending on the substance, severity and individual patient condition.

On One Campus

How coordinated on-site services may support patient care

Having selected services on one campus may reduce some external transfers and support coordination between the teams involved in a patient's care.

1 · Emergency assessment

The Emergency Department operates 24/7, and patients are assessed according to clinical urgency.

2 · On-site diagnostics

Selected diagnostic services are available on-site; test availability and reporting times vary.

3 · Surgical coordination

When surgery is recommended, the team coordinates available theatre, anaesthetic, blood-bank and critical-care resources.

4 · Follow-up and rehabilitation

Physiotherapy, review consultations and dialysis may continue locally, depending on the care required.
