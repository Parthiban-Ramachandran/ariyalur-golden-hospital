Illustrative Cases

What treatment actually looks like, step by step

The cases below are illustrative examples written to show the care pathway at our hospital — how a patient moves between casualty, imaging, theatre, ICU and physiotherapy. They are not accounts of identifiable patients, and no individual's records have been used.

These examples describe typical treatment pathways and are shown for general information only. They are not a promise of any particular result — every patient is different, and outcomes depend on the individual diagnosis. Please read our medical disclaimer.
Emergency & Trauma Care · Orthopaedics

Night-time road accident with a thigh bone fracture

01

A working-age man is brought to casualty after midnight following a two-wheeler accident, with severe pain in one thigh and an inability to move the leg. Because the casualty, CT scanner, blood bank and operation theatres are in the same building, assessment and surgery are not delayed by a transfer to another city.

  1. 1

    On arrival

    • Casualty team receives the patient at the door
    • Airway, breathing and circulation stabilised
    • CT scan to rule out head and abdominal injury
    • Digital X-ray confirms a femur shaft fracture
    • Blood grouped and cross-matched in-house
  2. 2

    Treatment

    • Fracture fixation in the surgical block
    • C-Arm imaging guides implant placement during surgery
    • Anaesthesia and intra-operative monitoring
    • Post-operative recovery in the intensive care unit
  3. 3

    Recovery

    • Physiotherapy begins from the ward
    • Graduated weight bearing as the fracture unites
    • Review X-rays at scheduled follow-up visits

Orthopaedics · Physiotherapy

Years of knee pain treated with joint replacement

02

An elderly woman has struggled for years with advanced knee arthritis — unable to climb the steps at home, sleeping poorly and managing on painkillers. A planned admission allows the whole pathway, from pre-operative fitness checks to rehabilitation, to be completed at one hospital close to her family.

  1. 1

    Assessment

    • Orthopaedic consultation and X-ray review
    • Blood investigations and ECG for fitness
    • Physician and anaesthetic clearance
    • Insurance or scheme approval arranged before admission
  2. 2

    Treatment

    • Total knee replacement in the surgical block
    • In-house blood bank support available
    • Monitored post-operative care
    • Pain relief and infection prevention protocol
  3. 3

    Recovery

    • Physiotherapy from the day after surgery
    • Walking practice with support, then independently
    • Home exercise plan explained in Tamil to the family

Urology · Scans & Imaging

Sudden severe flank pain from a kidney stone

03

A farmer arrives at the outpatient desk with waves of severe pain in one side of the back, nausea and blood in the urine — a presentation that needs imaging the same day, because an obstructed kidney cannot safely wait.

  1. 1

    Investigation

    • Urine and kidney function tests in the laboratory
    • Ultrasound scan to look for obstruction
    • CT scan to locate and size the stone
    • Urology consultation on the same visit
  2. 2

    Treatment

    • Pain control and hydration on arrival
    • Endoscopic stone surgery where the stone will not pass
    • Stenting to relieve obstruction when required
    • Day-care or short-stay admission depending on the procedure
  3. 3

    Follow-up

    • Repeat scan to confirm the kidney is draining
    • Advice on fluid intake and diet to reduce recurrence
    • Kidney function monitored at review

Nephrology · Dialysis Unit

Long-term dialysis without leaving the district

04

A man with long-standing diabetes reaches end-stage kidney disease and is advised maintenance dialysis. For families in Ariyalur the practical problem is rarely the treatment itself — it is travelling to another district several times a week, and paying for it. A local unit with scheme coverage changes both.

  1. 1

    Assessment

    • Kidney function and electrolyte testing
    • Nephrology consultation and dialysis planning
    • Vascular access arranged before starting
    • Screening bloods before the first session
  2. 2

    Ongoing care

    • Maintenance haemodialysis on a fixed weekly schedule
    • Anaemia, blood pressure and diabetes managed alongside
    • Government dialysis scheme paperwork handled by our staff
    • Direct booking line for slot changes
  3. 3

    What it changes

    • No repeated out-of-district travel for each session
    • Family can continue working and caring at home
    • Emergency support available on the same campus

Obstetrics & Gynaecology · Paediatrics

A labour that had to become an emergency caesarean

05

A first-time mother is admitted in labour. Monitoring during labour shows the baby's heart rate is no longer reassuring, and the decision is made to deliver by caesarean section. The value of a hospital with a theatre, blood bank and paediatric cover on site is measured in minutes.

  1. 1

    During labour

    • Continuous maternal and foetal monitoring
    • Ultrasound assessment where needed
    • Obstetrician and anaesthetist called in
    • Theatre and blood bank readied
  2. 2

    Delivery

    • Emergency caesarean section
    • Newborn received by the paediatric team
    • Mother monitored closely after surgery
  3. 3

    Afterwards

    • Breastfeeding support and wound care
    • Newborn checks before discharge
    • Immunisation and post-natal review scheduled

Emergency Medicine · Intensive Care

Pesticide poisoning brought in within the hour

06

Poisoning is a recurring emergency in an agricultural district, and survival depends heavily on how quickly treatment starts. A patient is brought to casualty soon after ingestion, and treatment begins immediately while the family is still completing admission formalities.

  1. 1

    Immediate care

    • Airway protected and breathing supported
    • Decontamination and antidote as indicated
    • Urgent bloods and monitoring started
    • Admission to intensive care
  2. 2

    In the ICU

    • Continuous cardiac and respiratory monitoring
    • Ventilatory support where required
    • Repeated laboratory testing to guide treatment
    • Physician review through the night
  3. 3

    Before discharge

    • Step-down to the ward as the patient stabilises
    • Psychiatric consultation offered where appropriate
    • Family counselling and follow-up plan

The Common Thread

Why keeping everything on one campus matters

In every case above, the thing that saves time is not a single machine — it is that casualty, imaging, laboratory, blood bank, theatre and intensive care are a short corridor apart.

1 · Seen without delay

Casualty is staffed round the clock, so a patient who arrives at 2 a.m. is assessed at 2 a.m.

2 · Diagnosed on site

MRI, CT, X-ray, ultrasound and the laboratory are in-house, so the diagnosis does not wait on an outside centre.

3 · Treated here

Three operation theatres, an intensive care unit and an in-house blood bank mean surgery can start when it needs to.

4 · Recovered locally

Physiotherapy, review consultations and dialysis continue in the same district, close to the family.
