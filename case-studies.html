How Care May Work

How patient care may progress

The examples below are simplified, general descriptions of patient care — the stages a patient may move through, from assessment to possible care and follow-up.

Emergency & Trauma Care · Orthopaedics

Illustrative patient care after a road accident with a suspected fracture

01

A patient is brought to casualty after a road accident with severe pain in one leg. On-site services may reduce the need for external travel. Transfer may still be required depending on the patient's condition and available resources.

  1. 1

    Assessment

    • The casualty team assesses and stabilises the patient
    • The clinical team may request X-rays, CT or other tests according to the injuries suspected
    • Blood grouping and cross-matching where required
  2. 2

    Possible care

    • Fracture treatment may be conservative or surgical, depending on the injury
    • Anaesthesia and intra-operative imaging support surgery when performed
    • Post-operative monitoring is provided in the recovery area, ward or ICU according to clinical need
  3. 3

    Follow-up

    • Physiotherapy may begin during admission when clinically appropriate
    • Mobility and weight-bearing progress according to the surgeon's and physiotherapist's instructions
    • Review appointments and repeat imaging as advised

Actual investigations and treatment are decided by the treating clinician after individual assessment.

Orthopaedics · Physiotherapy

Assessment and treatment options for advanced knee arthritis

02

A patient with long-standing knee pain is assessed for advanced arthritis. Treatment options range from medication and physiotherapy to joint replacement, and are chosen with the patient after individual assessment.

  1. 1

    Assessment

    • Orthopaedic consultation and imaging review
    • Preoperative assessment and investigations are selected according to the patient's health, planned procedure and anaesthetic requirements
    • The insurance desk can assist with documentation; admission authorization remains subject to insurer or scheme approval
  2. 2

    Possible care

    • Non-surgical options such as medication and physiotherapy may be advised first
    • Joint replacement surgery where clinically indicated, with the risks and alternatives discussed beforehand
    • Monitored post-operative care, pain relief and infection-prevention measures
  3. 3

    Follow-up

    • Physiotherapy begins when clinically appropriate. Recovery and mobility goals vary between patients
    • Exercise instructions are explained to the patient and family in a language they understand, where language support is available
    • Review consultations as advised by the treating team

Actual investigations and treatment are decided by the treating clinician after individual assessment.

Urology · Scans & Imaging

Illustrative patient care for suspected kidney stone pain

03

Severe flank pain, fever, vomiting or blood in the urine requires prompt medical assessment. The clinician will determine whether urgent imaging is needed.

  1. 1

    Assessment

    • Clinical assessment of pain, fever and urinary symptoms
    • Urine tests, blood tests, ultrasound or CT may be recommended after clinical assessment
    • Specialist availability should be confirmed
  2. 2

    Possible care

    • Treatment may include pain relief, observation, medication, drainage or a procedure, depending on the stone and the patient's condition
    • A urologist may recommend a procedure when clinically indicated. The available options, risks and alternatives should be discussed with the patient
    • Day-care or inpatient admission depending on the treatment chosen
  3. 3

    Follow-up

    • Repeat imaging or kidney-function testing where advised
    • General advice on fluid intake and diet from the treating clinician
    • Review appointments as recommended

Actual investigations and treatment are decided by the treating clinician after individual assessment.

Nephrology · Dialysis Unit

Local maintenance dialysis services for eligible patients

04

Regular dialysis can create medical, financial and travel challenges for patients and families. Eligible scheme coverage may reduce treatment costs, subject to current rules and authorization.

  1. 1

    Assessment

    • Kidney-function and related investigations
    • Nephrology consultation and dialysis planning
    • The nephrology team plans dialysis access and timing according to clinical urgency and individual circumstances
  2. 2

    Possible care

    • Maintenance haemodialysis on an agreed schedule
    • Related health conditions are monitored and managed by the appropriate treating clinicians
    • Hospital staff assist with eligible scheme documentation and authorization requests
  3. 3

    Follow-up

    • Local appointments may reduce travel for some patients, depending on the required services and specialist follow-up
    • Referral for tertiary review or access surgery where clinically indicated
    • Dialysis enquiries — operating hours vary. Call 84899 26941

Actual investigations and treatment are decided by the treating clinician after individual assessment.

Obstetrics & Gynaecology · Paediatrics

Illustrative patient care for an unplanned caesarean birth

05

When urgent delivery is recommended, coordination between obstetric, anaesthetic, theatre and newborn-care teams is important.

  1. 1

    Assessment

    • Maternal and fetal monitoring is provided according to clinical assessment and labour-care protocols
    • Ultrasound or other assessment where indicated
    • The relevant obstetric, anaesthetic and newborn-care professionals are contacted according to the clinical situation and current availability
  2. 2

    Possible care

    • Caesarean delivery when recommended by the obstetric team
    • Newborn assessment and care by the available newborn-care team
    • Post-operative monitoring of the mother
  3. 3

    Follow-up

    • Breastfeeding support and wound care
    • Newborn checks before discharge
    • Immunisation and post-natal review as advised

Actual investigations and treatment are decided by the treating clinician after individual assessment.

Emergency Medicine · Intensive Care

Illustrative emergency patient care after suspected pesticide exposure

06

Suspected poisoning is a medical emergency. Seek urgent professional help and bring the product container or label only if it is safe to do so. Call 99438 27233 or the appropriate public emergency service.

  1. 1

    Assessment

    • Do not induce vomiting or attempt home treatment unless instructed by a qualified poison-information or emergency-care professional
    • Airway, breathing and circulation assessed on arrival
    • Urgent investigations and monitoring as the clinical team decides
  2. 2

    Possible care

    • Monitoring, antidotal treatment, respiratory support, ward admission, ICU care or referral may be required depending on the substance and severity
    • Repeated laboratory testing to guide treatment where indicated
    • Transfer to another centre if the required level of care is not available
  3. 3

    Follow-up

    • Step-down care as the patient stabilises
    • Emotional-health assessment and psychosocial support may be offered when appropriate, with respect for the patient's circumstances and privacy
    • Family guidance and a follow-up plan on discharge

Actual investigations and treatment are decided by the treating clinician after individual assessment.

On One Campus

How coordinated on-site services may support patient care

Having selected services on one campus may reduce some external transfers and support coordination between the teams involved in a patient's care.

1 · Emergency assessment

The Emergency Department operates 24/7, and patients are assessed according to clinical urgency.

2 · On-site diagnostics

Selected diagnostic services are available on-site; test availability and reporting times vary.

3 · Surgical coordination

When surgery is recommended, the team coordinates available theatre, anaesthetic, blood-bank and critical-care resources.

4 · Follow-up and rehabilitation

Physiotherapy, review consultations and dialysis may continue locally, depending on the care required.
